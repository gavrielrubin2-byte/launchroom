Launchroom is a real-time feedback board for launch day. A small team gets shared channels (first impressions, bugs, feature ideas) where everything users say lands live, with a presence strip showing who is online, emoji reactions, and read receipts. One click on the Insights page runs Claude over the whole board and clusters the feedback into themes with sentiment and representative quotes, and a built-in AI chat assistant answers questions grounded in the actual feedback records.

DeepSpace features used: Records for channels, messages, reactions and read receipts; realtime sync so new feedback appears instantly in every open session; presence for the online strip; a server action that runs the Claude clustering analysis; the AI Chat assistant with server-side record tools; built-in auth with Google sign-in; and DeepSpace deployment for hosting.

Main tradeoff: I kept one shared message room per app instead of a separate room per channel. That lets the AI features see every message without extra plumbing, at the cost of less isolation if the app ever grew to multiple teams.

How it was built: an AI coding agent (Instinct) designed, built and deployed the app end to end on my DeepSpace account, including debugging a room-scoping issue that hid messages from the AI features and switching the assistant to Haiku after Opus hit the free tier's billing limit. I verified the deployed app myself: signing in, posting messages across channels, the presence strip, the insights analysis, and the assistant's grounded answers. Known rough edge: the assistant's model picker still says Opus even though the server pins Haiku.
